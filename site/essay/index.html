Draft essay for practitioners · from the working paper

Your judgment is the product. Where does it live?

P. Sarode and R. Nair · Future of Ways of Working, Theoremlabs.io · 5 min read

In short. Expert engagements run on chat, file shares and slides, none of which know who confirmed what. Agents now draft findings in minutes, so "who decided" gets blurrier. We propose a workspace per engagement where agents draft and one named person confirms, and we want to test whether you and your clients would trust it.

The engagement you already run

You interview a dozen people at a client. You read what documentation exists. You form a view and write it up. Weeks later, someone asks which interview a conclusion came from, which findings you examined yourself, and where the client's confidential material is now. Most firms can't answer quickly.

The most valuable part of the work is the part no tool records.

What agents change

Agents can interview, transcribe and draft. In a field study of 758 consultants, access to a model improved speed and quality on tasks inside its ability, and made people less likely to get the right answer on a task just outside it. The edge of the model's ability isn't visible to the person using it.

That removes much of the junior work, and it raises a harder question than speed: when an agent drafts a finding and a person forwards it, who decided?

Three questions your client will ask

Who owns what? What the client's people said belongs to the client. Your method belongs to you. Where does it live, and who can reach it? Including which model services see it. How can you prove it? In the language their security team already uses.

If a workspace can't answer these in the system itself, not just in a contract, a careful client won't let an agent interview its people.

Draft, Confirm, Commit

Our proposal is a simple pattern. Agents draft. You confirm, or change, every record. When you commit, the client sees the result read-only, with each finding traceable to its source interview. Here is one record, with invented content, as it would look:

Nothing becomes authoritative until a named person confirms it, and no fork of the software can remove that step.

After the pyramid

If agents absorb the junior tasks, the staffing pyramid thins. We describe a smaller team: a Lead who is accountable and the only one who confirms, and an Operator who runs the room. The open worry is apprenticeship. Juniors used to learn judgment by doing the work the agents now do.

What we don't know

This is a position paper. We have no field data. We state seven hypotheses, for instance how often leads override an agent's score, and whether interviews change conclusions that documents alone would reach. If the answers are "rarely" and "no", the idea is weaker than we think, and we will say so.

Run one real mission with us

We are looking for five to eight practitioners to run a single engagement in a Mission Room. In return: a co-authored case study and a say in what gets built. Writing a playbook needs your expertise, not code.
